    It took around two days to design this blog. I had the idea for a Terra and Serra split for a long time but never managed to have it realised until now.

    The main driver for that split has bloomed from the quite obvious relation between flowers and poetry through the root of the word Anthology. As opposed to Serra where I would build that Anthology; Terra would stand to contain everything that was out of my control. This split would be reflected in the contents as poetcy and prophecy.

    However, while I was building these pages I noticed that this split was missing an element of the earth: the sea. The sea often gives me a sense of belonging I cannot find anywhere else so I wanted to have it have a place here as well. With that, I added Mare to the pot with note that since navigation is a prophecy of what is to come of the horizon it should assume that role and Terra should be reserved for a representation of the freedom bound to flesh in the day to day dealings.

    In the end I have three legs:

    • Terra for pondering
    • Serra for poetry
    • Mare for prophecy

    which is satisfactory in its own way as "kamuğ üç adaklığ emitmez bolur / üçegü turur tüz kamıtmaz bolur"1

    I am used to playing around with words that connect through roots, rhymes, or vague alliterations to scratch an itch through bringing about a twisted composition that at the end amounts to lame wordplay but starting a design from scratch is always more challenging.

    In poetry, parody is often a great place to start as it lays down some ground to build upon. I would argue that is one of the major reasons "Immature poets imitate; mature poets steal."2 I intended to follow suit and looked for inspiration in the works of others. I wanted simplicity so I mostly looked for blogs of retired academics with nothing but simple html pages but I lack their established weight and I realised I cannot do without some form of flair so that did not do it. Luckily, for me, around the beginning of August, I had just come upon the blog of a young researcher who fascinates me and that helped me with the direction of my design, especially in the about page.3

    I tried to pay attention to detail in the design. The sine wave in the about page is supposed to represent the ups and downs, the background stars are a live map of the sky from around Istanbul —which is close enough to the anchor point for me—, and I even designed distinct brightness toggles for the three different blog pages. I am kind of proud of the work I put into this.
